How the ECO62 egg is simulated

This is the ECO62 design, a separate design from the DexArm simulator. The parent body is one egg made of two bodies held by magnets: the gel chamber at the wide end and the tail at the narrow end. The RealMan ECO62 rides folded inside the tail. The 20 November simplifications hold: a hard shell, no rings or petals, a plain birth opening at the stern of the gel chamber, two pumps under the gel chamber, no contractions; the arm pushes the newborn out while air goes in; the newborn is the Kresling spindle; the electronics kit is placed once. The docking face and the birth opening are two different places.

Every number comes from stage3_eco62.json, written by Python scripts in bnm_design/stage3_eco62_tail/. The browser replays the joint angles and the boom position of every frame and computes the forward kinematics of the official URDF chain itself; the tip readout compares that with the target. The clock is the real process clock; the view is a time lapse.

The egg

Circular sections, 1.80 m from the tail tip to the stern, widest 0.89 m. The gel chamber holds about 100 L of support gel below the waterline. The docking face is a flat bulkhead at the joint with a 500 mm port above the water and nine N42 magnets in three arcs below the port. The stern tip is cut off by a plane: the plain birth opening, 300 mm across, wholly above water. The rim is the 4 mm PETG wall itself with a 2 mm rounded lip, flush with the shell: the body passes the rim with more than 100 mm to spare, so the rim carries no birth load, and the wall near the stern only sees the small water and air pressure of the hull. The egg floats at the waterline found by bisection on the hull; a stainless trim weight in the gel chamber bilge makes the docked egg level with the arm folded. Alone, the tail floats at the same waterline with water ballast in the keel tanks of the tail, so the two faces meet. Both joints are thin and flush with the shell. The docking faces are 6 mm PETG plates, 10 mm only where the nine N42 discs (20 x 8 mm) sit flush in pockets, with a 2 mm rounded lip at the port: the largest load on the joint, the arm reaching to the stern with the newborn, is about 30 N m, carried by 157 N of magnet hold on a lever of about 0.6 m, which bends a 6 mm plate to about 6 MPa against roughly 50 MPa for PETG.

The arm

The ECO62 stands upright on the carriage of a two stage telescopic boom that runs along the floor of the tail and on into the gel chamber through the docking port, above the gel. Folded, the arm fits a 279 x 151 x 168 mm box; with the tool it rides in a 371 mm long space in the tail. The tool is a straight 220 mm tube: a valve block and a gripper at the flange end, a 6 mm push tube and a 12G needle that stands 40 mm out. Every pose is solved with the full six axis kinematics, with the boom carriage as a seventh axis that follows the tool and keeps the joints away from their limits; unfolding and folding are joint space paths found by a tree search and checked against the tail, the boom and the arm itself.

Printing and birth

The kit comes in with the tail and goes onto the seat cups in one pick and place. The skin path is the Stage 3 G-code, turned to lie with the nose toward the stern; the needle prints straight down. After the cure the needle goes into the valve boss at 60 degrees and lifts the folded newborn out of the gel. Then the arm pushes the newborn along the egg axis out of the stern opening while inflating: every story stays folded inside the gel chamber and opens as that story passes the rim, nose first; the air is metered to the push position (at most 2 L/min) and the push waits while the air catches up; the last story opens as the tail end leaves the rim, a short top up follows, and the newborn finishes in the water. The inflation model is the Stage 3 bar and hinge model, unchanged. The newborn geometry comes from one file, baby_current.json in the design folder, so a later newborn can replace the present one. The arm force is the gel bearing drag while the body moves up through the gel plus the weight of the body once out. Out of the opening, the needle lets go, the newborn falls into the water, floats and swims away on two jets from the kit pumps.

Limits

  • Collisions are checked on sampled mesh points (260 per link) frame by frame; distances are good to about 1 mm.
  • The arm and the tail are wet where they meet the water; waterproofing is ignored, as decided.
  • Link masses inside the ECO62 are split by mesh volume for the trim; the total is the datasheet 3.6 kg.
  • The inflation path between the two stress free states is a straight line; the order of opening is indicative.
  • The fall of the newborn uses a simple vertical model with broadside drag; spray and pitching are ignored.
