A simplified parent body, after the sketch

The sketches ask for a tail with the arm at its very front, the rack, the tools and the kit store, and every other part in line inside a long pointed taper; and a head, the gel chamber, that is a bare shell with a snout sticking out in front. The gel lies in one small bowl in the head, with a flat surface and one straight ramp that runs from the bowl floor out through the snout to the lip. The snout is a third printed piece, bonded to the head. The docking seam is dry. The Rotrics DexArm stands on a fixed base in the tail and reaches into the gel chamber. Nothing slides. The parent body may rest on the tank floor; only the newborn floats. This page follows that logic and calculates every dimension: the reach and the payload of the arm, the forces, the inflation and the print volume of the Bambu Lab H2S. Where the sketch does not hold, the page says so and shows the smallest change that works.

    How this is simulated

    A Python script (bnm_design/simple/design.py) builds the hull, the newborn and the arm, plans every move of the cycle, and writes simple.json; the browser replays and interpolates. The arm uses the link meshes and the kinematics of the Stage 2 DexArm model with the official joint ranges; each pose on the timeline is solved by inverse kinematics, and a pose outside the ranges is reported as a failed check. The newborn is the folded body of the full size study, resized: Kresling stories with sixteen radial star pleats, opened by air and finished by a 25 percent stretch of the skin. While the arm pushes, the body is a rigid body on a pin at the valve: the pitch is the smallest pitch at which the body clears the gel chamber and the rim, and the part outside droops until the water carries that part or the hull stops the droop, with a lag. The three slender stories at the tail end open inside the snout; each wider segment opens after the segment has passed the lip, with a lag as well. After the nozzle lets go, the newborn is a rigid body in the vertical plane with gravity, buoyancy from forty slices, drag in heave, pitch and surge, a contact at the lip, the thrust of the two kit pumps and the ballast water; the roll is a damped oscillator with the righting moment of the calculated metacentric height. The clock shows process time; the view is a time lapse.

    Colliders: the two shells (the inner surface as a function, the wall as a 3 mm band), the rim of the opening, the rack forks, the kit store, the seat, the bulkhead in the tail, the eight arm links (their mesh vertices), the module head and stem, the magnet, the parked modules, the kit as a box, and the newborn as a dense point set in every state. Every frame of the timeline is tested for every pair; the Checks panel lists the deepest overlap of each pair. The ball needle in the valve and in the well of the kit, the print needle in the work, the magnet on the kit and the kit in the newborn are contacts by design and are left out.

    The tank

    Staging

    One long glass tank on a dark plinth in a black room, one lamp straight above. The water is nearly still: faint, slow caustics and a quiet surface. The switch above the view adds a few slow inner lights in the parent body and the newborn; the default is off. None of this changes the physics.
