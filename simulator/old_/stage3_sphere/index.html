How this is simulated

Stage 3 follows the decisions of 20 November 2026. The mother robot is a plain hard shell with one opening and six magnets around the opening. The support gel simply fills the lower shell. Two water pumps sit under the hull. The tail docks to the opening under water. The arm places one electronics kit, prints the folded Kresling baby around the kit, then pushes the baby out through the same opening while air goes in through the needle. The newborn rises, floats and swims on two small jets.

The clock shows modeled process time. Docking, inflation, release and swimming replay the recorded Stage 3 calculation. The browser reads the print path and solves the arm pose at each displayed position. Comparisons with a physical prototype require measured pressure, force and buoyancy.

Where the arm sits

One opening serves the dock and the birth, and the arm has to push the baby out through that opening, so the arm lives inside the mother robot, in the air dome above the gel. The DexArm stands on a 140 mm rail on a shelf just above the gel surface; the rail keeps every point of the print between 260 and 420 mm from the arm base, where the DexArm reaches with a 200 mm stem. The ECO62 option hangs from a rail under the roof, because standing upright the ECO62 is taller than the dome. While docked, the tail brings silicone, air and power through pads in the face; the tail backs away before the birth so the opening is clear.

Floating

The shell has one opening below the waterline, so the hull works like a diving bell. The whole body (shell, parts, gel, the air in the dome) floats when the water displaced by the outer hull, with the opening closed by a plane, weighs as much as the body. The script finds that waterline by bisection on the hull shape. The inner gel surface is set by the gel volume; the dome air then sits at the water head between the outer waterline and the gel surface. Trim ballast puts the center of gravity over the center of buoyancy, so the hull floats level. The tail is a free flooding fairing with a sealed pod; the pod is sized so the tail floats at the height of the opening with a thin mast through the surface. The newborn floats where the water displaced by the inflated body equals skin, kit and the gel trapped inside. Heave, pitch and roll motions are not simulated; the bodies sit at their static waterlines.

Docking

Planar motion of the tail (surge, sway, yaw) with added mass, quadratic drag, two pump thrusts from a pump curve, a speed loop and line of sight steering. In the last 20 mm the six magnet pairs take over: pull from the datasheet value of one N42 20 x 8 mm disc (91 N at contact) with a fitted fall off, centering from the datasheet shear. The tail meets 3 mm silicone bumpers, so the magnets never close below 4 mm. The mother recoils freely in surge.

Printing

The toolpath uses the design speeds of 20 mm/s for deposition and 30 mm/s for travel. It records extrusion volume in mm³; machine feed rates and syringe calibration are separate inputs. The baby body comes from the body plan of the Stage 3 electronics study: an eight sided Kresling spindle with a smooth waist band for the kit. Each Kresling story has two stress free states, printed short and deployed long, with the twist angles chosen so both diagonal crease families keep their length. The folded body lies sideways in the gel and is sliced into 1.5 mm layers; the G-code is that slicing, with the arm base on a 140 mm shelf rail (axis A). Every point was checked with the DexArm inverse kinematics ported from Stage 2. The kit goes in first, in one pick and place: the encapsulation saddle of the kit sits on three cups on a small seat and forms the belly of the waist band. A vertical needle cannot reach under an object, so the skin under the kit is the saddle itself, not print. The skin is then printed bottom up around the kit, laps onto the saddle lip and closes over the top. Every pass of the stem was checked against the kit parts. The recorded toolpath extrudes 91.0 mL, about 97 g at the design density. The buoyancy calculation assumes a 148 g skin. The 51 g difference remains to be reconciled by weighing a finished print. The stories alternate in hand (left, right), as the electronics study chose, so the band and the kit do not roll while the body inflates.

Push and inflate

The rise trace starts at release and runs while the arm withdraws. During the rise, the display interpolates body shape, inner volume and internal pressure between the release and surface states.

Inflation uses a bar and hinge model of the folded skin (Dragon Skin 10 NV, E 319 kPa, 1.6 mm wall): stretching of every crease edge and bending of every crease, measured from the printed shape. Air is isothermal; the gel trapped inside the printed body stays inside. For each step the script minimizes elastic energy plus the work of the outside water pressure, minus the free energy of the air, so the stories open in the order the physics gives, and snap where the energy has a barrier. Stories still inside the gel pay the work of pushing gel aside. Once a story reaches the deployed stop, the skin itself stretches like a thin tube. The arm force is the yield drag of the part still in the gel (sphere factor 9.3 on the front area, shear on the sides) plus rim friction from the lift of the part already out. A force loop slows the push and the air when the force nears 85 percent of the arm payload: 0.5 kg for the DexArm, 2 kg for the ECO62.

Newborn

The released body rises under buoyancy against broadside drag and added mass, settles at the floating draft, and swims on two jets. Jet thrust is mass flow times jet speed, with the flow found where the pump curve meets the jet head. Drag uses the submerged front area and a drag coefficient of 0.8. The mother then swims on the same model with two larger jet pumps.

Limits

  • Pump curves, part masses and drag coefficients are estimates of the part class; each is marked in the data file. Replace them with weighed parts and measured curves.
  • The inflation path between the two stress free states is a straight line in story height and twist; a real story can take another path. The Kresling barrier and the order of opening are therefore indicative.
  • Gel yield is a single yield stress (25 Pa) with no rate effect and no healing time. The wake of the stem in the gel near fresh bead is not modeled.
  • Waterproofing of the arm, the kit and the opening is ignored, as decided. Arm link collisions with the shell are checked only on the drawn meshes, frame by frame.
  • The ECO62 shares the DexArm path and timing; only the push speed and force limit change. The ECO62 is heavier; the waterline is solved for the DexArm.
  • When the electronics study writes the kit file, the page draws every part from the file; the physics keeps the first option until the script is run again.

Open problems

  • The magnets snap the tail in from about 20 mm and the tail meets the bumpers at about 0.3 m/s. Weaker or switched magnets (electropermanent, as in Stage 2) would make the click gentle.
  • The gel inside the printed body stays inside as ballast (about 0.6 L), so the newborn floats low. Pumping the gel out through the kit pumps after birth would raise the newborn.
  • With the ECO62, the forearm passes through the rear face at the end of the push; a shorter push or a different elbow posture is needed.
  • Gel at 25 Pa is a compromise: high enough to hold the kit and the bead, low enough for the DexArm to push the body out.
