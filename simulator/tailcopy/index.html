A new tail, born in two halves, and an arm built in it face to face

The end effector of the parent has two tools. With the gel line on the print needle it prints; with the holder (an electromagnet, a ball needle and a folding crank) it picks things up and sets them where they belong. The head of the quilted version prints the two halves of a new tail, each a quilted double membrane: N, the nose half, folded flat over its pods (battery and Raspberry Pi, the pump), and F, the face half, with the arm's seat plate printed into its floor. The tail splits where the hard tail is already printed in two on the H2S. Each half is born the way a head is born: the lengthwise folds open, the holder and then the crank push the rim, the strip drapes over the lower lip to the tank floor and each section fills only once it lies outside; the parent backs away and the rest slides out. N docks to F: the new tail is whole, without an arm.

Then the parent's tail leaves its head, turns round and docks face to face with the new tail. Its arm reaches into the new tail as it reaches into a head. It brings the four modules of a new arm (base, upper arm, forearm, end effector) from a tray on the back glass, one at a time, and builds the arm upright in F's seat. From the base on, the new arm has power from N's battery and holds each joint where the parent can reach it. Once it has an arm, the new tail picks up the rest itself: the last thing on the tray it takes with its own arm.

Point by point

Notes

    How this is simulated

    A Python script (build.py, with armfk.py) reads the quilted head's quilt.json and arm.json, solves every reach of the parent's arm with the DexArm's kinematics and official joint ranges, searches the joint angles the new arm must hold for each join, and writes every frame to tailcopy.json. The birth path (bowl floor, chin, lip, drape) is the same in the script and the page. The browser replays and interpolates, with the scene, the light and the materials of the quilted head simulator.

    Limits

    • The water stands at 270 mm so the parent can carry the modules under water (its 500 g payload cannot lift the 1.4 kg base in air): the quilted head then floats unless its low cells take gel, and the hard tail needs steel to keep its wheels on the floor.
    • The tank is 3.0 x 1.22 m, larger than the quilted head's, so a tail fits beside a tail and one can turn.
    • Module masses, the sealed base, the snaps and the drape are estimates; the section shapes during a birth are interpolated, not solved as a membrane.
