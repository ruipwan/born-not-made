A new tail, printed whole and born once, and an arm put together for it

Every body here is a quilted double wall: two skins of Dragon Skin 30 joined by webs along the body, only the space between them inflated, the floor a flat double skin. The parent's tail is built that way from the first frame, like the head of the quilted version; the DexArm stands on a printed seat plate on its floor, the store on a rack on the same plate, two wheels run on the glass through slots in the floor skin.

The head prints a new tail whole, in one print: the parent's tail copied, one quilted body from nose to face cuff, folded flat as the head is (laid flat, Z folded 3 across and 3 along, 18 layers). The pack is a little longer than the head's own and no taller; the flat floor of the bowl takes it (see the checks). It is born the way the head's newborn is born, nose first: the keel opens the lengthwise folds, the holder and its folding crank push the rim, each row of the strip opens and fills only once it lies outside, the parent backs away. The new tail lies in front of the head, its open face toward it, empty.

The parent moves its head aside to the port glass, releases it and goes round it. It takes the parts of a new arm from a tray at the starboard glass, one trip each: the base (in a sealed shroud, on a drive frame with two wheels), a power pack of three 18650 cells, the upper arm, the forearm, the end effector. Each is taken by the holder, the magnet on a steel pad of the part's grip boss and the ball needle in its socket, lifted straight, carried, and set down on the assembly plate beside the new tail's face, or clicked onto the joint the new arm, already powered, holds out. The finished arm folds and drives itself into the new tail, where its frame pins into sockets printed into the floor. Then the parent docks face to face and fills the new tail's circuits with gel: the new body's only ballast. With its own arm the new tail takes a holder from the tray and slides a tile set into a pocket on its own flank.

Point by point

Notes

    How this is simulated

    A Python script (build.py, with quiltgen.py, tcbody.py and armfk.py) reads the quilted head's quilt.json and arm.json. It builds the two skins of every body by the construction of the head, computes the folded pack and the birth keyframes of the new tail along a path laid on the head's own skins, solves every pose of both arms with the DexArm's kinematics and joint ranges, searches the join poses of the new arm and the grip bosses of the parts, and tests every frame for penetration (sampled points against the space between two skins, boxes and the glass). It writes every frame to tailcopy.json. The page replays and interpolates, with the scene, the light and the materials of the quilted head simulator.

    Limits

    • The births are kinematic, as the head's: rows move along a fixed path and are interpolated between flat, folded and filled; the rear of a newborn settling onto the floor is interpolated; nothing is solved as a membrane.
    • The drives of the tails and of the new arm are kinematic, without slip or drag.
    • Module masses are a split of the DexArm's 2.4 kg; the shroud of the base, the latches of the sockets, the magnet on a bare pad, friction and gel drag are estimates (see the checks).
    • The tank is made to order, 2.72 x 0.86 m outside, larger than the quilted version's, so the head can stand aside and a tail can turn.
