Society, touch and birth

It starts with two robots and a few loose tails. Two that relate touch at the opening. A body with a tail holds the arm, so it prints the newborn in its gel chamber. Only the translucent silicone head is born: it fills with air like a balloon as it is pushed out through the opening, leaves the body already full size and swims off. The tails, with the arm inside, are a fixed number and are never born. After a birth the tail lets go, and another head takes it from behind, so the few arms pass from body to body.

Rules

Relation is shared memory, likeness of body and time spent near each other. Every body is printed a little differently: its proportions, rings around the body, a crest, a lobed section, the tone of the clay, the print texture (straight layers, a spiral, crosshatch, waves, dimples or smooth, with its own pitch and depth) and how clear or frosted the silicone head is are inherited and drift. A robot ready to bear looks for the one it relates to most; at least one of the two has a tail. They touch opening to opening and exchange memory and form. The one with a tail carries: the arm prints the newborn in gel, layer by layer, then pushes it out through the opening while filling it with air, so it leaves the body full size and does not grow afterwards. Each newborn mixes both bodies, with drift. Only the translucent silicone head is born. The tail holds the arm and is never born: there is a fixed number of tails in the tank, two on the first robots and the loose ones. A head without a tail swims to a loose tail and docks it from behind. After a birth the tail lets go of the one that carried and floats free, and another head takes it, so the few arms are shared around the tank and whoever holds one can carry next. Nobody dies; the water feeds only so many, so births slow as the tank fills.
