Society

A fixed number of tailed ones stay in the tank. Each tailed one holds an arm and gel. Every other body is born from a tailed one and has no tail. The bodies swim out, read the light, the stream and the crowd, and come back to tell. A tailed one keeps what is told as lines in the head. With enough lines, of enough kinds, the tailed one prints the next generation folded in the gel, then pushes the newborn out through the mouth while a pump fills the newborn with air. What was told shapes the next generation.

Rules

The cast. The tailed ones are fixed in number and do not move far. A tailed one is two rounded parts: a clear quilted head of silicone with gel on the floor, and a tail holding a Rotrics DexArm at true size. Only a tailed one can make a body. Every body without a tail was born in this tank from one of the tailed ones, and no body without a tail makes another. A body is born small, about half its adult length, and grows over two to three minutes to roughly twice that, never longer than 0.85 of a tailed one. About one body in five keeps away: it roams the far side of the tank and comes back to tell only when it has a lot to say.

The water. Sixteen small lamps hang over the tank. A patch of low lamps moves slowly across the grid, so a bright pool does not stay bright. A stream runs through the tank in a band that turns slowly; small motes on the surface show where the water moves. A body gains charge in light and shares the light with every body close by. Swimming, a strong stream and a large body all cost charge. In the dark a body without a lamp feels less far.

Reading and telling. A body swims to a place, reads the light, the stream and the crowd there, and keeps the reading as a memory. A body holds only a few memories; the oldest drops out. Two robots closer than the talk radius may talk, a few turns each. A turn says one thing: a reading, how the body fared and what the body lacks, a trace handed down, a question, a request. A listener keeps what was said, so word travels from body to body. A body low on charge asks where light is and swims to the place named. A tailed one sends a body to read a place the store has no fresh word of.

The store. A tailed one keeps what is told as lines, drawn as thin loops stacked in the head, and a ring on the water fills as the lines add up. A new line counts as one, a line that changes an old one counts as one, a line already held counts for little, and word passed on by a second speaker counts for less than a first reading. To generate, a tailed one needs the set number of lines, of at least three kinds, from at least two speakers. At the start no robot has anyone to ask, so a tailed one makes a first generation from three lines read at the tailed one's own place.

The next generation. The tailed one weighs the store: word of strong streams pulls toward a narrower, faster body; word of darkness toward a lamp and a longer reach; word of crowds and of bodies that faded toward a body slower to drain; lost memories toward more room for traces. Every trait shares one budget, so no body is best at everything. Chance adds a little, set by Mutation. Shape follows function: a fast body is long and narrow, a lasting body is round and large, a body that holds much has a full back, a body with a lamp is clear and glows.

The form. Every body without a tail is a soft pillow of quilted silicone, after the Birth variation diagram: one smooth closed outline, filled with air. The back is round and blunt; the front ends in one to four short round points with smooth notches between them, and every point ends in a small open nozzle with a rolled rim. The forms are the diagram's: the plain drop, two points, three points, bent upward, a point at each end, one point turned, a fan of small points, one long and one short. Each first tailed one has a head of its own in this family and starts a line from that form. The form is handed down: each generation takes the form of the generation before with small changes of its own (the proportions, the depth of the notches, the length and the angle of the points, a slight bend), and now and then the line moves to a neighbouring form of the diagram. What the tailed one was told nudges the change. Word of strong streams makes the body longer and narrower and favours fewer points; word of crowds and of fading makes the body rounder and the points shorter; word of darkness lifts the front toward the lamps, makes the body flatter and wider, and favours more points; lost memories make the back fuller. So each line drifts a different way, and the tree is not even. These links are fiction, a guess: more points might help a body steer.

Birth. The tailed one prints the newborn folded flat in the gel, layer on layer, with the needle of the arm, the points laid in along the body. Then the arm pushes the folded pack to the mouth while the pump fills the newborn with air: the part past the lip swells like a balloon, the part still inside stays folded, the points fill last, and the newborn leaves at full size. A generation is one to three newborns of one design. At the birth the store is compacted. A few lines stay with the tailed one; the rest go with the newborn as traces, with one note on where light is and what to stay out of.

Feedback. A body that fits the water stays charged, lives longer and tells more, so the shape that works is heard most. A body that runs out of charge lets the air go, sinks and fades. A body near enough to see tells of the fading; if none saw, the tailed one of that line notes that the body did not come back.

The dialogue model follows the artist's Raised by many (Cognitive Agents, 2026), where each agent knows only what the agent read or was told, and a full memory is compacted at a birth. Here the lines are composed on this page from each speaker's state, without a language model.
