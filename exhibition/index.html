The exhibition

Born, Not Made as a room to walk through: a ring of projection, four installations from simple to complex, and the people who argue about what to call what they see.

The idea

AI is now so capable that one day a next generation may follow. As software the answer is easy: a copy. A body cannot be copied that way. A new body has to come from somewhere, and so far only living beings have ever made a new body from their own.

Robots will surely find rules of their own in the end. Until then, life is the one reference there is. So the work studies how many kinds of living beings give birth: what each new generation inherits, and what new thing appears that lets a species keep changing. What is learned goes into a way for a robot to do the same.

The robot is there to serve the idea. The final work takes the form of art: an exhibition with a series of installations, which can also become part of a performance. People witness the whole birth of an intelligent robot. Some are moved, or find the newborn cute. Others find the scene unsettling, or read a warning in it. Someone who walks in sure that nothing in a machine is alive may walk out less sure. The work is an open debate about what we are willing to call birth, and what we are willing to call life.

The setup

A dark round room, about 17 m across. The wall is one ring of projection, 4 m tall, open at one side where people come in. Four glass tanks stand on black plinths in an arc, each under one lamp, from the smallest and simplest to the largest. Each tank holds one parent body and shows one way a new body can leave another. The tanks are the props; the room, the light and the people are the piece.

The path

  1. EnterThe ring is already alive: on the wall, bodies of many sizes drift, meet and bring out new ones, each stretch of wall in a different way.
  2. Fill and releaseThe smallest tank. A folded body, made beforehand, takes air and is pushed out. Nothing is printed. Is this already a birth?
  3. Print and releaseA needle draws a new body in gel inside the parent body. Water pushes the new body out.
  4. Print, fill, growThe printed body leaves folded and wrinkled, swims straight ahead and grows as air opens the folds.
  5. A body that can bear the nextThe largest tank. The newborn carries parts, and a third body already begins inside the second.
  6. The ring againPeople stand in the middle and talk. The question on the wall is the one they are already asking each other.

From simple to complex

Life has many ways to bring a new body out of another. The installations answer a few of them in order, starting with the least a machine can do. The wall shows the same ladder as moving drawings, one way of birth for each stretch of the ring.
