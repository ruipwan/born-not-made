The exhibition

Born, Not Made as a performance in a dark room: one birth at a time, from simple to complex, three walls that remember each one, and two visitors whose view changes.

The idea

AI is now so capable that one day a next generation may follow. As software the answer is easy: a copy. A body cannot be copied that way. A new body has to come from somewhere, and so far only living beings have ever made a new body from their own.

Robots will surely find rules of their own in the end. Until then, life is the one reference there is. So the work studies how many kinds of living beings give birth: what each new generation inherits, and what new thing appears that lets a species keep changing. What is learned goes into a way for a robot to do the same.

The robot is there to serve the idea. The final work takes the form of art: an exhibition with a series of installations, which is also a performance. People witness the whole birth of an intelligent robot. Some are moved, or find the newborn cute. Others find the scene unsettling, or read a warning in it. Someone who walks in sure that nothing in a machine is alive may walk out less sure. The work is an open debate about what we are willing to call birth, and what we are willing to call life.

The story, in six beats

The piece is told through two visitors. One walks in sure that a machine is only a machine. The other is drawn in from the start. The room gives the two one birth at a time, each a little harder to dismiss than the last, and the walls keep every newborn. Select a beat to jump there.

    How the room works

    One thing at a time. The room is almost dark. Only one lamp is up, over the tank where a birth is happening. The visitors follow the light from the smallest tank to the largest, as one group, the way a procession moves from station to station.

    Nothing is taken away. Each newborn stays afloat in the tank after the lamp moves on. By the last beat four of them share the room with the visitors.

    The walls and the tanks are one event at two scales. A tank shows one birth, up close. The wall behind shows the same birth large: the breath of the pump as widening rings, the path of the needle as a stack of drawn layers. When the newborn leaves the parent body, the drawing lets go as well and becomes one more body on the wall. In the last act the bodies on the wall begin to bring out new ones without any tank, and spread from the front wall over the two side walls until the visitors stand inside a society.

    The setup. A dark room about 14 m wide and 10 m deep. A front wall and two side walls carry one continuous projection, 4.2 m tall; the fourth side is open and people come in there. Four glass tanks on black plinths stand in a row before the front wall, the smallest at the left. A full ring of projection in a round room is a possible variant of the same piece.

    From simple to complex

    Life has many ways to bring a new body out of another. The four acts answer a few of them in order, starting with the least a machine can do, so that the first question arrives early: is this already a birth?

    In lifeWhat happensIn the room
    DivisionOne body becomes two of the same size.Not built. This is the software answer: a copy.
    BuddingA small body grows on the side of a large one and lets go.Act 1. A ready made body is filled and released.
    LayingThe new body leaves early and finishes outside.Act 2. Drawn inside the parent body, pushed out, finished by the water.
    CarryingThe new body forms inside and leaves when able to live alone.Act 3. Printed folded, filled, released, grows while swimming.
    One for manyOne body bears the young for a whole colony.Act 4 and the walls: bodies that bear the next, at scale.
