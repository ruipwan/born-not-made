Print and place, in one process

The newborn is not printed first and fitted with electronics later. The parts are joined into one kit before the print starts: a Raspberry Pi 5, the sensors, a motor driver, two water pumps, a micro air pump, a cell and a regulator, wired on one plate and cast in silicone. Then one arm does everything inside the head. It prints a layer of the newborn's wall in the gel, changes its tool, picks the kit up, presses it into the wet layer, changes back, and prints over it. When the print is done, the parts are already inside, and the newborn can move.

The gel is what makes this simple. A print in a gel bath needs no supports, so nothing has to be cut away, and the soft body slides out whole. Because nothing has to be held from below, a small desktop arm is enough. The arm is a Rotrics DexArm, which takes interchangeable heads on one mount: a printing head, an air pick with suction cups, and a soft gripper, with a rotary module as the fourth axis.

Numbers

  • Kit pod 156 x 88 x 27 mm, as on the wall page; the folded print is 143 x 194 mm in plan.
  • Raspberry Pi 5, 85 x 56 mm; regulator 22.9 x 22.9 x 9.7 mm; two pumps of 42.6 x 23.9 x 22.7 mm; one 18650 cell.
  • Bead about 1.5 mm, one layer shown here; the real print stacks many.
  • DexArm: payload 500 g at most, repeatability 0.05 mm; the pneumatic rotary module carries 350 g, suction cups of 5, 10 and 15 mm, soft gripper range 30 mm.

Assumptions

  • The size of the micro air pump (30 x 12 x 12 mm) and of the sensor boards is assumed.
  • One layer stands for the whole wall; the layer height and path are drawn, not sliced from the real model.
  • The pod weighs about 0.5 kg in air, over the 350 g of the air pick; in the gel it weighs far less. Untested.
