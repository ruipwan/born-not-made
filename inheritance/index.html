How does one robot teach the next?

The film follows one encounter at a time. Its simulation replays a longer history of visitor, peer and water cues to train each robot’s own model. Each of the three models has the same 19 cues and seven possible actions. A cue and the action learned for it count as one pattern once the model predicts that action with at least 60% probability.

Which encounters become lessons? In this artwork, the teacher chooses from ten learned patterns and gives priority to the cues it encountered most often. The first robot selects two patterns (20%). The second selects one (10%). These percentages set the teaching budget for this story. Each teacher keeps its own weights and experience.

What does the student learn? For each selected cue, the teacher predicts a probability for each of the seven actions. The student updates its own weights to match the teacher’s softened distribution (temperature 2). After teaching, it can respond independently and train on its own encounters.

The model runs in this page. The Raspberry Pi 5 8GB drawn inside each shell is the proposed onboard computer. The film compresses time. Training on the physical robots has yet to be tested.
