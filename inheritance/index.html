In 3D

Open the 3D simulation · the full page adds the readouts, the assumptions and the open questions.

The kit · One aluminum plate carries a Raspberry Pi 5 (85 x 56 mm; needs a supply of 3 A at 5 V to boot, typical bare board draw 800 mA, Raspberry Pi documentation), fed 5 V on GPIO pins 2 and 4 by a Pololu S13V30F5 regulator (2.8 to 22 V in, 5 V, 3 A, 22.9 x 22.9 x 9.7 mm, 85 to 95 % efficient), one 18650 cell (3500 mAh, 3.6 V, as in simulator/doublewall/quilt.json; the Samsung sheet could not be opened), sensors on I2C (GPIO 2 and 3) with STEMMA QT leads (4 pin JST SH, 1.0 mm, Qwiic compatible) and on I2S (GPIO 18 to 21), a TI DRV8833 dual H bridge (2.7 to 10.8 V, 1.5 A RMS a bridge, inputs high from 2 V, so the 3.3 V GPIO drives it) on GPIO 12 and 13 (PWM0, PWM1), and the two AITIAO 3 to 4.5 V micro submersible pumps the artist owns (0.18 A, about 100 L/h, 42.6 x 23.9 x 22.7 mm, about 30 g; no maker sheet exists, figures as recorded from a reseller page). A GPIO pin cannot run a pump: on RP1 boards all GPIO together supply at most 50 mA. The pumps run from the cell through the driver. Power: Pi 4.0 W / 0.9 + two pumps 2 x 0.18 A x 3.7 V = 1.3 W, so 5.8 W; one cell holds 3.5 Ah x 3.6 V = 12.6 Wh, about 2.2 h with both pumps on. The 500 mAh cell of the earlier kit would last about 19 minutes and would have to give about 1.6 A. Sizes of the sensor boards are assumed.

Sources · Valentine et al., Hybrid 3D printing of soft electronics, Adv. Mater. 2017 (parts picked and placed onto the printed matrix) · Bhattacharjee et al., Writing in the granular gel medium, Sci. Adv. 2015 (the gel yields at the tip and turns solid again behind it) · Hinton et al., ACS Biomater. Sci. Eng. 2016 (silicone and Carbopol do not mix) · Wehner et al., Nature 2016 (a premade controller cast in place, its inlets plugged) · Lai et al., J. Micromech. Microeng. 2019 (partly cured layers bond when cured together) · Smooth-On, Dragon Skin series technical bulletin (Dragon Skin 30: pot life 45 minutes, cure 16 hours; tin, latex and sulfur stop the cure).

Open questions · Heat: the Pi 5 turns about 4 W into heat inside silicone; Raspberry Pi rates the board for 0 to 70 °C and throttles the cores from 80 °C, harder at 85 °C. The aluminum face toward the water is the only path out; untested. Size: the pod is 156 x 88 x 27 mm; it fits the folded print in plan (143 x 194 mm) but adds about 23 mm to the 79 mm stack, more than the 17 mm of gel over it. The flat wall is 3.4 mm, so the pod is a thick island. Weight: about 0.5 kg in air, at the 500 g limit of the DexArm, so a dry magazine may be out of reach; in water the pod weighs far less. Radio: Wi Fi and Bluetooth through silicone and water reach only a short way. Charging, by coil or by contacts, is open. No source places a part inside a gel bath during a silicone print. Fresh Dragon Skin 30 may not bond to the cured pod, so the flange holes lock it. A gel film may stay on the pod. The potting must hold no tin or sulfur. The holder must let go without lifting the pod. Water may reach the parts through silicone over months. The capped tubes are untested. A Compute Module 5 (55 x 40 x 4.7 mm) would make the pod much smaller.
