# Where does the body end?

First research and production pass. The prior script took the first independent correction as the criterion of birth. That measure privileges motor competence. A newborn can be entirely dependent. Remove that test from the narration as a definition of who counts. Keep passive controls in the studio to understand our own images.

## A material observation

J. Nawroth and colleagues measured juvenile jellyfish in different temperature regimes. At this scale, water adhering to adjacent lobes can bridge the gaps during paddling. The effective moving surface comprises both tissue and surrounding liquid. This is a result for these animals under specific flow conditions. A much larger silicone machine cannot simply claim the same mechanism. Examine the original video and velocity fields for timing and geometry: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2880069/

The visually productive mismatch: a seam opens and separate contours become visible, yet some fluid continues to travel with the departing body. When have we actually seen two individuals? Anatomical separation, shared fluid motion, and beginning a distinct history give different answers. Compare Gilbert, Sapp and Tauber on the plurality of criteria for biological individuality: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23397797/ . Do not imply a robot–water system is a microbial symbiosis.

## First shots to test

Begin almost motionless with a grazing light on the submerged silicone fold. Leave a glass edge or adhered bubble in the frame as a stationary reference. Let contact flatten the soft body locally. On release, first one pleat recovers, then another; water and wall motion decouple by degrees. Choose the camera distance from the real prototype dimensions. Use faint suspended tracers sparsely near that gap, each anchored in the velocity field. No particle fountain, outline illustrations, interface arrows, or captions explaining what the lens has not shown. Lock exposure, lens and tank geometry across the separation. End with a second generation occupying altered water before any first action. Keep causality honest if motion is a numerical approximation.

Study Jean Painlevé's seahorse film for how the observation is staged, what is afforded by glass and how long an observer waits for a birth. https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/science-is-fiction-inside-the-wondrous-early-films-of-jean-painleve/ . Use original footage only if rights are established. The working question for the sound edit: what remains audible in the room after mechanical support ceases?

## A membrane has a time scale

Preston and colleagues measured the effect of a water-channel protein on the osmotic permeability of frog oocytes. A visible outline can stay recognizably similar while the rate of passage across it changes. Ripley's pipefish study asks a different question: how pouch fluid changes during gestation. Taken together they suggest two separate shots for a future tank shoot. Hold on the rim to see passage. Then stay with the carrier long enough to see whether the conditions it can maintain have changed. The robot's bladder is hydraulically inflated, so describing the recorded release as osmosis would substitute a metaphor for its mechanism. [Preston et al., 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1373524/) · [Ripley, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19447186/)

In *Water Beings*, a previous generation changes the space available to the next when remnants accumulate. In the workshop tank a technician can remove the remnants and reload the same child. That difference merits a place in the exhibition. Resetting promises each visitor an equivalent encounter, yet erases the material history that makes the digital generations distinct. For a mock-up, leave the last three actual reset durations visible. The next visitor inherits at least this small history of the room. Never invent past cycles for a fresh installation. [Artist's account of Water Beings](https://ruipengwang.space/water-beings)

## Duration as a curatorial choice

Ukeles's proposed exhibition *CARE* made repeated servicing part of the exhibition's duration. For this installation that raises a practical authorship question: if the person who retrieves and reloads the child is visible in the work, who decides how their work is represented and who pays for that time? Offer the operator the choice of visibility, agree credit and staffing, and place maintenance records where visitors can read them without obstructing the service route. This remains one curatorial trial. [Ukeles, Manifesto for Maintenance Art, 1969](https://trap.no/uploads/Ukeles_MANIFESTO.pdf)

Use Akerman's *Jeanne Dielman* to study how a fixed frame lets a repeated gesture gather force. In the workshop footage a ball becomes exposed millimetre by millimetre. Moving the lens during that interval would give the film an expressive movement the apparatus had never performed. The 4K detail therefore holds still until the channel opens. The return to the wider frame gives the visitor back the carrier, which is easy to forget when tracking the departing child. [Fondation Chantal Akerman, B. Ruby Rich interview archive](https://chantalakerman.foundation/archives/b-ruby-rich-on-jeanne-dielman/)
